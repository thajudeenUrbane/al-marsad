al-Marṣad · a brief for reviewing historians · September 2026

Where is this wrong?

We are designing a database for Islamic history. It is not built yet. This page explains the one idea behind it, and asks you to find the mistakes while they are still cheap to fix.

We are not asking you to approve a finished thing. We are asking you to attack an unfinished one. Ten minutes should be enough to decide whether the idea is sound.

The problem we are trying to solve

Almost every digital history project stores facts, and adds sources as footnotes:

The Hijrah took place in Rabīʿ al-Awwal, year 1.

Source: Ibn Hishām

This looks reasonable. But four things are lost, and none of them can be recovered later:

  1. When two reports disagree, somebody has to choose one.The database never records that a choice was made, or who made it.
  2. A grading has nowhere to live.You do not grade a fact. You grade a report. If the report is not stored as an object, the grading has nothing to attach to.
  3. The time gap disappears.A claim about year 1 first written down in year 220 is not the same kind of claim as one written down in year 90. A flat model cannot show the difference.
  4. Correcting it means rewriting the history.Rather than re-deriving it.

What we propose instead

Store the report — the khabar, with its chain. Build everything else from it.

Report (khabar) ──▶ chain ──▶ narrators, with their dates and places
      │
      ├─▶ the claim it makes — about a person, event, place, relationship
      │
      ├─▶ graded by — a named authority, in a named book, at a named page
      │
      ▼
   the history a reader sees — a RESULT, not an entry

The reader still sees "the Hijrah took place in Rabīʿ al-Awwal, year 1." But that sentence is now a result. The reports are still underneath it. Change a grading, and the result changes. Nobody rewrites the history, because the history was never typed in.

What this means for your work

Your grading stays yours
There is no "grade" field on a report anywhere in this system. There is only: this scholar, in this book, at this page, gave this grade, for this reason. Another scholar's different grading sits beside it. Neither erases the other.
Disagreement is kept, not settled
Three reports name three different people who brought food to the cave. All three stay, with their evidence. The system does not choose — and does not assume the three answers rule each other out.
Sīra is not forced into ḥadīth categories
Much sīra-maghāzī material was never meant to meet ḥadīth criteria. It has its own category. It is neither dismissed nor promoted.
Different traditions keep different scales
Sunni, Twelver Shia, Ibadi and academic assessments each use their own vocabulary. The system is forbidden from converting between them, or averaging them into one number.

What the system refuses to do

This is the part most worth checking. Refusals are where a system's honesty actually lives.

It will notWhy
Decide whether a chain is connectedMuttaṣil or munqaṭiʿ is your call, not a machine's
Grade anythingIt records gradings made by people
Guess a date, place or routeAn unknown stretch of the Hijrah route is drawn as a gap, never as a smooth line
Merge two similar names into one personAutomatic matching may only suggest. A person decides, and signs it
Rank Companions by any calculationNot offered as a feature, in any form
Let AI publishAI reads texts and proposes. A qualified person must accept it, and their name stays on it permanently

Where you would come in

The specification marks twenty decisions it refuses to make on its own. Among them:

  • Is this chain connected?
  • Is this man's companionship established, or disputed?
  • Are these two names one narrator, or two?
  • Which calendar conversion applies to this region and century?
  • Does including a report in a Ṣaḥīḥ collection count as an explicit grading?
  • In this mawlā tie, who is the patron and who is the client? The sources often do not say — and it must never be guessed from the order of names

What you decide is recorded under your name, permanently. It stays open to revision by whoever comes after you. And it can be cited.

What is actually finished

Being plain about this matters more than impressing you.

The data model Written, and machine-checkable. 1,804 statements. It validates.
The historical data Almost none. One example event — and it is clearly marked as unverified.

That order is deliberate. If we gathered data first and designed the model afterwards, the model would be shaped by whatever we happened to collect, and every mistake listed above would already be baked in.

Tell us where this is wrong.

Section 14 of the specification already lists eight places we think it is wrong — including whether we have chosen the right standard class for a report, and whether one report can really be given a single ascription when its text mixes prophetic material with a narrator's own comment.

If you would rather look at one file than read a specification, read the worked example. It is a single event modelled all the way down: the chain, the grading, an unresolved dispute, and a route segment that admits it is guessing.
