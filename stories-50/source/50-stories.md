# 50 Stories of the Prophet Muhammad ﷺ and His Companions

Oct 10, 2026 · @Thajudeen Aboobaker

## Introduction

These 50 stories follow the life of the Prophet Muhammad ﷺ in rough chronological order, from the first revelation in Makkah to his passing in Madinah. After every story is a list of **Companions connected to this story**, naming each Companion (Sahabi or Sahabiyyah) who took part in it and what role they played.

A few conventions keep the lists consistent. People who opposed Islam at the time of the story but later became Muslim are included, marked *(accepted Islam later)*. People who never became Muslim, such as Abu Jahl or Abu Lahab, appear in the story text but not in the Companion lists. Where scholars differ on a detail, the story says so briefly.

May Allah send His peace and blessings upon the Prophet ﷺ, and may He be pleased with all of his Companions (radiyallahu anhum).

## Part One: The Makkan Period

### 1. The First Revelation in the Cave of Hira

At about forty years of age, the Prophet ﷺ used to retreat to the Cave of Hira on Jabal al-Nur to worship. There the angel Jibril came to him and commanded, "Read!" He replied, "I am not a reader." After the angel pressed him three times, the first verses of Surah al-Alaq were revealed. He returned home trembling and said to Khadijah, "Cover me, cover me."

Khadijah comforted him, saying Allah would never disgrace him because he kept family ties, helped the weak, honoured guests and supported people in hardship. She took him to her cousin Waraqah ibn Nawfal, a Christian scholar, who recognised the signs of prophethood and said his people would drive him out.

**Companions connected to this story**

- **Khadijah bint Khuwaylid** — his wife; comforted him, affirmed his character, and became the first person to believe in him.
- **Waraqah ibn Nawfal** — Khadijah's cousin who confirmed the revelation; he died soon after, and many scholars count him among the believers.
- **Aishah bint Abi Bakr** — the main narrator of this hadith in Sahih al-Bukhari, as she heard it from the Prophet ﷺ.

### 2. The First Believers

After Khadijah, the first to accept Islam were Ali ibn Abi Talib, a boy living in the Prophet's household, Zayd ibn Harithah, his freed servant and adopted son, and Abu Bakr, his closest friend, who believed without hesitation. Abu Bakr then invited his own friends, and five of the ten promised Paradise entered Islam through him.

**Companions connected to this story**

- **Khadijah bint Khuwaylid** — first of all believers.
- **Abu Bakr al-Siddiq** — first free adult man to believe; brought others to Islam.
- **Ali ibn Abi Talib** — first child to believe.
- **Zayd ibn Harithah** — first freed slave to believe.
- **Uthman ibn Affan** — accepted Islam through Abu Bakr's invitation.
- **Al-Zubayr ibn al-Awwam** — accepted Islam through Abu Bakr.
- **Abd al-Rahman ibn Awf** — accepted Islam through Abu Bakr.
- **Sa'd ibn Abi Waqqas** — accepted Islam through Abu Bakr.
- **Talhah ibn Ubaydillah** — accepted Islam through Abu Bakr.
- **Bilal ibn Rabah** — among the earliest to accept Islam.
- **Umm Ayman (Barakah)** — the Prophet's nurse from childhood, among the first believers in his household.

### 3. The Call from Mount Safa

When Allah revealed "And warn your nearest kin" (26:214), the Prophet ﷺ climbed Mount Safa and called each clan of Quraysh by name. He asked, "If I told you an army was behind this hill, would you believe me?" They said, "We have never known you to lie." He said, "Then I am a warner to you of a severe punishment." His uncle Abu Lahab cursed him, and Surah al-Masad was revealed in reply.

**Companions connected to this story**

- **Abdullah ibn Abbas** — narrator of the hadith in al-Bukhari and Muslim.
- **Ali ibn Abi Talib** — present at the related gathering of Banu Abd al-Muttalib, where he alone stood up in support of the Prophet ﷺ.
- **Fatimah bint Muhammad** and **Safiyyah bint Abd al-Muttalib** — addressed by name in the related warning: "Save yourself from the Fire; I cannot avail you anything before Allah."

### 4. Bilal and "Ahad, Ahad"

Bilal was enslaved by Umayyah ibn Khalaf, who laid him on burning sand at midday with a heavy rock on his chest, demanding he reject Islam. Bilal only repeated, "Ahad, Ahad" (One, One). Abu Bakr bought him and set him free, and went on to free several other tortured believers.

**Companions connected to this story**

- **Bilal ibn Rabah** — endured the torture; later the first mu'adhdhin of Islam.
- **Abu Bakr al-Siddiq** — bought and freed him.
- **Amir ibn Fuhayrah** — another tortured slave freed by Abu Bakr.
- **Zinnirah al-Rumiyyah** — freed by Abu Bakr; she lost her sight under torture and Allah restored it.
- **Al-Nahdiyyah and her daughter** — freed by Abu Bakr.
- **Umm Ubays** and **Lubaynah** — enslaved women freed by Abu Bakr.

### 5. The Family of Yasir

Yasir, his wife Sumayyah and their son Ammar were tortured by Banu Makhzum under Abu Jahl. Passing by, the Prophet ﷺ said, "Patience, O family of Yasir, your promised meeting place is Paradise." Abu Jahl killed Sumayyah with a spear, making her the first martyr of Islam; Yasir also died under torture. When Ammar was forced to say words of disbelief, Allah revealed that one compelled while his heart is firm in faith is excused (16:106).

**Companions connected to this story**

- **Sumayyah bint Khayyat** — first martyr in Islam.
- **Yasir ibn Amir** — martyred under torture.
- **Ammar ibn Yasir** — survived; the verse of compulsion was revealed about him.

### 6. Abdullah ibn Mas'ud Recites at the Ka'bah

The Companions wanted someone to recite the Qur'an aloud to Quraysh. Abdullah ibn Mas'ud, a small man with no powerful clan, volunteered. He stood at the Maqam and recited Surah al-Rahman until Quraysh beat him. He returned bruised and said he was ready to do it again the next day.

**Companions connected to this story**

- **Abdullah ibn Mas'ud** — first to recite the Qur'an publicly in Makkah after the Prophet ﷺ.
- The **early Companions gathered with him**, who warned him he had no clan to protect him.

### 7. Hamzah Accepts Islam

Abu Jahl insulted and harmed the Prophet ﷺ near Safa. When Hamzah returned from hunting, a freedwoman told him what had happened. Furious, he went to Abu Jahl at the Ka'bah, struck him with his bow, and declared, "Will you insult him when I follow his religion?" He then embraced Islam fully, and Quraysh became more cautious.

**Companions connected to this story**

- **Hamzah ibn Abd al-Muttalib** — the Prophet's uncle; "Lion of Allah."

### 8. Umar ibn al-Khattab Accepts Islam

Umar set out with his sword to kill the Prophet ﷺ. Nu'aym ibn Abdullah told him his own sister had become Muslim. Umar burst into her house, where Khabbab was teaching her and her husband Surah Ta-Ha, and struck her. Seeing her blood and steadfastness, he asked to read the page. Moved, he went to Dar al-Arqam and accepted Islam. The Prophet ﷺ had prayed, "O Allah, strengthen Islam with one of the two Umars." After this, Muslims prayed openly at the Ka'bah.

**Companions connected to this story**

- **Umar ibn al-Khattab** — accepted Islam; later called al-Faruq.
- **Fatimah bint al-Khattab** — his sister, whose firmness moved him.
- **Sa'id ibn Zayd** — her husband, one of the ten promised Paradise.
- **Khabbab ibn al-Aratt** — was teaching them the Qur'an and hid in the house.
- **Nu'aym ibn Abdullah al-Nahham** — redirected Umar to his sister's house.
- **Al-Arqam ibn Abi al-Arqam** — owner of the house where the Muslims met.
- **Hamzah ibn Abd al-Muttalib** — stood guard at the door when Umar arrived.

### 9. Ja'far Before the Negus of Abyssinia

To escape persecution, a group of Muslims migrated to Abyssinia under a just Christian king. Quraysh sent Amr ibn al-As and Abdullah ibn Abi Rabi'ah with gifts to bring them back. Ja'far ibn Abi Talib spoke for the Muslims, describing the ignorance they had left and the truth they had found, then recited from Surah Maryam. The Negus wept and refused to hand them over.

**Companions connected to this story**

- **Ja'far ibn Abi Talib** — spokesman before the Negus.
- **Umm Salamah (Hind bint Abi Umayyah)** — present; narrator of the account.
- **Abu Salamah ibn Abd al-Asad** — her first husband, among the emigrants.
- **Uthman ibn Affan** and **Ruqayyah bint Muhammad** — among the first emigrants to Abyssinia.
- **Asma bint Umays** — Ja'far's wife, among the emigrants.
- **Al-Zubayr ibn al-Awwam**, **Abdullah ibn Mas'ud**, **Abd al-Rahman ibn Awf**, **Mus'ab ibn Umayr** and **Uthman ibn Maz'un** — among the emigrants in the first or second migration.
- **Al-Najashi (Ashamah ibn Abjar)** — the Negus; later accepted Islam, and the Prophet ﷺ prayed the funeral prayer in absentia for him.
- **Amr ibn al-As** — Quraysh's envoy *(accepted Islam later)*.
- **Abdullah ibn Abi Rabi'ah** — the second envoy *(accepted Islam later)*.

### 10. The Boycott in Shi'b Abi Talib

Quraysh hung a pact in the Ka'bah to boycott Banu Hashim and Banu al-Muttalib: no trade, no marriage. For about three years the Prophet ﷺ and his family were confined to a valley, surviving on leaves and scraps. Some sympathetic Qurayshis worked to end it, and the document was found eaten by termites except for the words "In Your name, O Allah," just as the Prophet ﷺ had said.

**Companions connected to this story**

- **Khadijah bint Khuwaylid** — endured the boycott and spent her wealth during it.
- **Ali ibn Abi Talib** — inside the valley with the Prophet ﷺ.
- **Sa'd ibn Abi Waqqas** — reported that hunger drove him to soften and eat a dried piece of leather.
- **Al-Abbas ibn Abd al-Muttalib** — of Banu Hashim, confined with them *(accepted Islam later, by most accounts)*.
- **Hakim ibn Hizam** — Khadijah's nephew, who smuggled food to her *(accepted Islam later)*.
- **Zuhayr ibn Abi Umayyah** — helped tear up the pact *(accepted Islam later, according to some reports)*.

### 11. The Journey to Ta'if

After the deaths of Abu Talib and Khadijah in the Year of Sorrow, the Prophet ﷺ walked to Ta'if to invite Thaqif. Its leaders rejected him and set youths to stone him until his feet bled. Resting in a garden, he made his famous supplication. The angel of the mountains offered to crush the town, but he said, "I hope Allah will bring from their descendants people who worship Him alone." A Christian servant named Addas brought him grapes and believed.

**Companions connected to this story**

- **Zayd ibn Harithah** — accompanied him and shielded him from the stones, suffering head wounds.
- **Addas** — the servant from Nineveh who believed in him.
- **Aishah bint Abi Bakr** — narrator of the hadith of the angel of the mountains.

### 12. Al-Isra wal-Mi'raj

In one night the Prophet ﷺ was taken from Makkah to Jerusalem, where he led the prophets in prayer, then ascended through the heavens, where the five daily prayers were prescribed. When he told Quraysh, they mocked him and ran to Abu Bakr. Abu Bakr said, "If he said it, it is true," earning the title al-Siddiq.

**Companions connected to this story**

- **Abu Bakr al-Siddiq** — affirmed the journey immediately.
- **Umm Hani bint Abi Talib** — the Prophet ﷺ spent that night at her house, by many reports, and told her first.
- **Anas ibn Malik** and **Malik ibn Sa'sa'ah** — main narrators of the Mi'raj hadith.

### 13. The Pledges of Aqabah

In the pilgrimage seasons, groups from Yathrib met the Prophet ﷺ at Aqabah. Twelve pledged in the first year, and he sent Mus'ab ibn Umayr back with them as teacher. Through Mus'ab, the chiefs Sa'd ibn Mu'adh and Usayd ibn Hudayr accepted Islam. The next year seventy-three men and two women pledged to protect him as they would their own families, opening the way to the Hijrah.

**Companions connected to this story**

- **Mus'ab ibn Umayr** — first envoy and teacher of Islam in Madinah.
- **As'ad ibn Zurarah** — hosted Mus'ab and led the first group.
- **Sa'd ibn Mu'adh** — chief of Aws; accepted Islam through Mus'ab, and his whole clan followed.
- **Usayd ibn Hudayr** — Aws leader; accepted Islam through Mus'ab.
- **Al-Bara ibn Ma'rur** — first to pledge at the second Aqabah.
- **Ubadah ibn al-Samit** — present at both pledges; narrator of their terms.
- **Ka'b ibn Malik** — present at the second pledge and narrated it.
- **Abdullah ibn Amr ibn Haram** — Jabir's father, accepted Islam that night.
- **Jabir ibn Abdullah** — present at the second pledge as a young man.
- **Sa'd ibn Ubadah** — leader of Khazraj at the pledge.
- **Abu al-Haytham ibn al-Tayyihan** — among the twelve leaders (nuqaba).
- **Nusaybah bint Ka'b (Umm Umarah)** and **Asma bint Amr (Umm Mani')** — the two women who pledged.
- **Al-Abbas ibn Abd al-Muttalib** — accompanied the Prophet ﷺ to secure the terms *(accepted Islam later, by most accounts)*.

## Part Two: The Hijrah and Early Madinah

### 14. The Hijrah and the Cave of Thawr

Quraysh plotted to kill the Prophet ﷺ in his bed. Ali slept in his place under his green cloak, and the Prophet ﷺ left with Abu Bakr for the Cave of Thawr. When the trackers stood at its mouth, Abu Bakr whispered his fear, and the Prophet ﷺ said, "What do you think of two when Allah is the third?" (9:40). After three days they set out for Madinah. Suraqah ibn Malik chased them for the bounty, but his horse's legs sank into the sand three times; he asked for safety and was promised the bracelets of Kisra.

**Companions connected to this story**

- **Abu Bakr al-Siddiq** — companion in the cave and on the journey ("the second of two").
- **Ali ibn Abi Talib** — slept in the Prophet's bed, then returned the people's trusts before following.
- **Asma bint Abi Bakr** — carried food to the cave; tore her belt in two to tie the provisions, earning the name Dhat al-Nitaqayn.
- **Aishah bint Abi Bakr** — narrated the account of the departure.
- **Abdullah ibn Abi Bakr** — spent the nights in the cave and brought news of Quraysh.
- **Amir ibn Fuhayrah** — grazed sheep over their tracks and supplied milk; travelled with them.
- **Suraqah ibn Malik** — the pursuer, who later wore Kisra's bracelets in Umar's time *(accepted Islam later)*.
- **Umm Ma'bad al-Khuza'iyyah** — the woman whose dry goat gave milk when the Prophet ﷺ touched it; she gave the famous description of him *(accepted Islam later, by many reports)*.
- **Buraydah ibn al-Husayb** — met them on the road with his tribe and accepted Islam.

Abdullah ibn Urayqit, their hired guide, was not a Muslim at the time.

### 15. Arrival in Madinah and the Building of the Mosque

The people of Madinah went out every morning to wait for the Prophet ﷺ. When he arrived, children sang, and every family wanted to host him. He let his camel walk freely, saying "She is commanded." She knelt by a plot owned by two orphans, and he stayed at the nearby house of Abu Ayyub. The land was bought, and the Prophet ﷺ carried bricks alongside his Companions to build the mosque.

**Companions connected to this story**

- **Abu Ayyub al-Ansari (Khalid ibn Zayd)** — hosted the Prophet ﷺ for months.
- **Sahl and Suhayl ibn Rafi'** — the two orphans who owned the land.
- **As'ad ibn Zurarah** — their guardian, who had prayed on the plot.
- **Abu Bakr al-Siddiq** — paid for the land, according to reports.
- **Ammar ibn Yasir** — carried two bricks at a time; the Prophet ﷺ foretold, "The transgressing party will kill him."
- **Kulthum ibn al-Hidm** — hosted the Prophet ﷺ at Quba on arrival.
- **Anas ibn Malik** — a boy who narrated the joyful arrival.
- **Abdullah ibn Salam** — saw the Prophet's face and knew it was not the face of a liar (see story 19).

### 16. The Brotherhood of the Muhajirun and Ansar

The Prophet ﷺ paired each emigrant from Makkah with a helper from Madinah as brothers. Sa'd ibn al-Rabi' offered Abd al-Rahman ibn Awf half his wealth. Abd al-Rahman thanked him and said, "Just show me the way to the market." He traded and soon prospered.

**Companions connected to this story**

- **Abd al-Rahman ibn Awf** and **Sa'd ibn al-Rabi'** — the famous pair.
- **Salman al-Farisi** and **Abu al-Darda'** — brothers; Salman later advised him to balance worship with his family's rights.
- **Abu Bakr** and **Kharijah ibn Zayd** — paired as brothers.
- **Umar ibn al-Khattab** and **Itban ibn Malik** — paired as brothers.
- **Abu Ubaydah ibn al-Jarrah** and **Sa'd ibn Mu'adh** — paired as brothers.
- **Ammar ibn Yasir** and **Hudhayfah ibn al-Yaman** — paired as brothers, by some reports.
- **Mus'ab ibn Umayr** and **Abu Ayyub al-Ansari** — paired as brothers.
- **Anas ibn Malik** — the brotherhood was declared in his family's house.

The pairings listed follow Ibn Ishaq and Ibn Sa'd; some lists differ.

### 17. Salman al-Farisi's Search for Truth

Salman was born a Zoroastrian in Persia. Drawn to Christianity, he served a series of monks; the last told him a prophet would appear in a land of palms, who would accept gifts but not charity and carry the seal of prophethood between his shoulders. Sold into slavery in Madinah, he tested the Prophet ﷺ on all three signs and accepted Islam. The Prophet ﷺ helped him buy his freedom by planting 300 palm saplings with his own hands.

**Companions connected to this story**

- **Salman al-Farisi** — the seeker.
- **Abdullah ibn Abbas** — narrated Salman's story as Salman told it.
- **The Companions who planted** the palms with the Prophet ﷺ, and those who contributed gold for his ransom.

### 18. The First Adhan

The Muslims discussed how to gather people for prayer: a bell, a horn or a fire. Abdullah ibn Zayd saw the words of the adhan in a dream. The Prophet ﷺ said, "It is a true vision. Teach it to Bilal, for his voice is stronger." When Umar heard Bilal, he ran out saying he had seen the same dream.

**Companions connected to this story**

- **Abdullah ibn Zayd ibn Abd Rabbih** — saw the adhan in his dream.
- **Bilal ibn Rabah** — the first to call it.
- **Umar ibn al-Khattab** — had seen the same dream.
- **Abdullah ibn Umar** — narrated the discussion about calling to prayer.
- **Abdullah ibn Umm Maktum** — later also served as mu'adhdhin with Bilal.

### 19. Abdullah ibn Salam Accepts Islam

Abdullah ibn Salam, a leading Jewish scholar of Madinah, asked the Prophet ﷺ three questions only a prophet would know, and was satisfied. Before announcing his Islam, he hid while the Prophet ﷺ asked his community about him. They called him their best and son of their best. When he came out and declared his faith, they called him their worst.

**Companions connected to this story**

- **Abdullah ibn Salam** — the scholar who believed; the Prophet ﷺ gave him glad tidings of Paradise.
- **Anas ibn Malik** — narrated the account in Sahih al-Bukhari.
- **Khalidah bint al-Harith** — his aunt, who accepted Islam with him, according to reports.

## Part Three: The Expeditions and Battles

### 20. The Battle of Badr

In Ramadan of 2 AH, 313 Muslims with two horses faced about 1,000 Quraysh. Before battle, the Prophet ﷺ consulted his Companions, and both Muhajirun and Ansar pledged to follow him even into the sea. He spent the night in supplication until his cloak fell from his shoulders. Allah sent angels in support, and Quraysh were defeated, losing seventy dead including Abu Jahl.

**Companions connected to this story**

- **Abu Bakr al-Siddiq** — stayed with the Prophet ﷺ in his shelter and comforted him during his supplication.
- **Umar ibn al-Khattab** — spoke in support before battle; narrated the Prophet's supplication.
- **Al-Miqdad ibn al-Aswad** — said, "We will not say as the people of Musa said; we will fight on your right and your left."
- **Sa'd ibn Mu'adh** — spoke for the Ansar; built the Prophet's shelter.
- **Al-Hubab ibn al-Mundhir** — advised camping at the last well.
- **Hamzah**, **Ali** and **Ubaydah ibn al-Harith** — fought the opening duels; Ubaydah was mortally wounded.
- **Mu'adh ibn Amr ibn al-Jamuh** and **Mu'awwidh ibn Afra'** — two young Ansar who struck down Abu Jahl.
- **Abdullah ibn Mas'ud** — found Abu Jahl dying and finished him.
- **Bilal ibn Rabah** — confronted his former torturer Umayyah ibn Khalaf, who was killed.
- **Umayr ibn al-Humam** — threw away his dates, saying the wait was too long, and fought until martyred.
- **Harithah ibn Suraqah** — young martyr; the Prophet ﷺ told his mother he was in al-Firdaws.
- **Sawad ibn Ghaziyyah** — see story 37.
- **Talhah ibn Ubaydillah** and **Sa'id ibn Zayd** — sent to scout the caravan; counted among the people of Badr.
- **Uthman ibn Affan** — stayed in Madinah by the Prophet's order to nurse Ruqayyah, who died; counted among the people of Badr.
- **Abu Lubabah ibn Abd al-Mundhir** — left in charge of Madinah.
- **Al-Abbas ibn Abd al-Muttalib** — fought with Quraysh and was captured *(accepted Islam later)*.
- **Abu al-As ibn al-Rabi'** — the Prophet's son-in-law, captured; Zaynab sent her mother's necklace as ransom *(accepted Islam later)*.

### 21. The Battle of Uhud

In 3 AH, Quraysh returned with 3,000 men. The Prophet ﷺ placed fifty archers on a hill with orders never to leave. When the Muslims began winning, most archers left for the spoils. Khalid ibn al-Walid circled behind, and the Muslims were thrown into confusion. A rumour spread that the Prophet ﷺ had been killed. He was wounded in the face and his tooth broken, while a small band of Companions shielded him.

**Companions connected to this story**

- **Hamzah ibn Abd al-Muttalib** — martyred by Wahshi's spear; called "Master of the Martyrs."
- **Mus'ab ibn Umayr** — carried the flag until both hands were cut off, then martyred; his shroud was too short to cover him.
- **Abdullah ibn Jubayr** — commander of the archers, who stayed at his post with a few and was martyred.
- **Nusaybah bint Ka'b (Umm Umarah)** — fought to defend the Prophet ﷺ and received many wounds.
- **Talhah ibn Ubaydillah** — shielded the Prophet's face with his hand until it was paralysed.
- **Sa'd ibn Abi Waqqas** — shot arrows as the Prophet ﷺ said, "Shoot, may my father and mother be ransomed for you."
- **Abu Dujanah (Simak ibn Kharashah)** — took the Prophet's sword "with its right" and used his back as a shield against arrows.
- **Abu Ubaydah ibn al-Jarrah** — pulled the helmet rings from the Prophet's cheek with his teeth, losing two front teeth.
- **Hanzalah ibn Abi Amir** — went out newly married without a ritual bath; washed by the angels after his martyrdom.
- **Qatadah ibn al-Nu'man** — his eye came out; the Prophet ﷺ put it back, and it became his better eye.
- **Abdullah ibn Amr ibn Haram** — martyred; Allah spoke to him without a veil, as the Prophet ﷺ told his son Jabir.
- **Amr ibn al-Jamuh** — though lame, insisted on fighting and was martyred.
- **Anas ibn al-Nadr** — see story 22.
- **Ali ibn Abi Talib**, **Abu Bakr**, **Umar** and **al-Zubayr** — among those who stayed near the Prophet ﷺ.
- **Fatimah bint Muhammad** — washed his wound and burnt a mat to stop the bleeding.
- **Aishah** and **Umm Sulaym** — carried water to the wounded.
- **Wahshi ibn Harb** — killed Hamzah *(accepted Islam later; see story 45)*.
- **Khalid ibn al-Walid** and **Ikrimah ibn Abi Jahl** — led the Quraysh cavalry *(both accepted Islam later)*.
- **Abu Sufyan ibn Harb** and **Hind bint Utbah** — led Quraysh *(both accepted Islam later)*.

### 22. Anas ibn al-Nadr: "I Smell Paradise"

Anas ibn al-Nadr missed Badr and vowed that if Allah let him witness another battle, Allah would see what he would do. At Uhud, when others wavered, he said to Sa'd ibn Mu'adh, "Paradise! By the Lord of the Ka'bah, I smell its scent beyond Uhud." He was found with more than eighty wounds, recognised only by his sister from his fingertips. The verse "Among the believers are men who were true to their covenant with Allah" (33:23) is linked to him.

**Companions connected to this story**

- **Anas ibn al-Nadr** — the martyr.
- **Sa'd ibn Mu'adh** — whom he addressed; Sa'd said, "I could not do what he did."
- **Al-Rubayyi' bint al-Nadr** — his sister, who identified him.
- **Anas ibn Malik** — his nephew, narrator of the story.

### 23. The Battle of the Trench (al-Khandaq)

In 5 AH, about 10,000 confederates marched on Madinah. On Salman's advice, the Muslims dug a trench across the open northern side in freezing cold and hunger. When a rock defeated them, the Prophet ﷺ struck it three times, and with each spark foretold the conquest of Syria, Persia and Yemen. After nearly a month, discord among the enemy and a fierce wind broke the siege.

**Companions connected to this story**

- **Salman al-Farisi** — proposed the trench; the Prophet ﷺ said, "Salman is one of us, the People of the House."
- **Jabir ibn Abdullah** and his wife **Suhaylah bint Mas'ud** — prepared a small goat and some barley that, by the Prophet's blessing, fed the whole army.
- **Ali ibn Abi Talib** — answered the challenge of the champion Amr ibn Abd Wudd and killed him.
- **Nu'aym ibn Mas'ud** — a new Muslim who sowed distrust between Quraysh and Banu Qurayzah.
- **Hudhayfah ibn al-Yaman** — crossed into the enemy camp on the coldest night to bring news.
- **Al-Zubayr ibn al-Awwam** — volunteered to scout; the Prophet ﷺ called him "my disciple (hawari)."
- **Sa'd ibn Mu'adh** — struck by an arrow in his arm (see story 24).
- **Safiyyah bint Abd al-Muttalib** — defended the women's fort against an enemy who approached it.
- **Hassan ibn Thabit** — was in the women's fort with Safiyyah.
- **Al-Bara' ibn Azib** — narrated the Prophet ﷺ carrying earth until dust covered his chest.
- **Abu Sufyan ibn Harb**, **Khalid ibn al-Walid** and **Ikrimah ibn Abi Jahl** — among the confederates *(all accepted Islam later)*.

### 24. The Death of Sa'd ibn Mu'adh

Wounded at the Trench, Sa'd prayed that he would not die until his heart was at peace regarding Banu Qurayzah, who had broken their treaty in the Muslims' most dangerous hour. Their case was referred to his judgment, which the Prophet ﷺ said matched the judgment of Allah. His wound then reopened and he died. The Prophet ﷺ said, "The Throne of the Most Merciful shook at the death of Sa'd ibn Mu'adh," and seventy thousand angels attended his funeral.

**Companions connected to this story**

- **Sa'd ibn Mu'adh** — chief of Aws.
- **Rufaydah al-Aslamiyyah** — nursed him in her tent in the mosque, the first field hospital in Islam.
- **Abu Lubabah ibn Abd al-Mundhir** — tied himself to a pillar of the mosque in repentance after a mistake during the siege until Allah accepted his repentance.
- **Jabir ibn Abdullah** — narrated the hadith of the Throne shaking.
- **Aishah** — described Sa'd's condition and the Prophet's grief.
- **Umm Sa'd (Kabshah bint Rafi')** — his mother, whom the Prophet ﷺ consoled.

### 25. The Treaty of Hudaybiyah

In 6 AH, the Prophet ﷺ set out with about 1,400 Companions for Umrah but was stopped at Hudaybiyah. He sent Uthman to negotiate, and when a rumour said Uthman was killed, the Companions pledged under a tree to stand firm. Quraysh then sent Suhayl ibn Amr, and a truce was signed with terms that seemed harsh to the Muslims. Allah called it "a clear victory" in Surah al-Fath, and within two years Islam spread more than ever before.

**Companions connected to this story**

- **Uthman ibn Affan** — the envoy; the Prophet ﷺ pledged on his behalf with his own hand.
- **All who pledged under the tree (Bay'at al-Ridwan)** — Allah declared He was pleased with them (48:18).
- **Salamah ibn al-Akwa'** — pledged three times at the Prophet's request.
- **Ali ibn Abi Talib** — wrote the treaty; the Prophet ﷺ himself erased "Messenger of Allah" when Ali would not.
- **Umar ibn al-Khattab** — questioned the terms in distress, later regretting it.
- **Abu Bakr al-Siddiq** — calmed Umar with the same answer the Prophet ﷺ had given.
- **Umm Salamah** — advised the Prophet ﷺ to shave his head first, after which all followed.
- **Abu Jandal ibn Suhayl** — escaped to the Muslims in chains and was returned under the treaty.
- **Al-Mughirah ibn Shu'bah** — stood guard over the Prophet ﷺ with a sword.
- **Abu Basir** — later escaped and formed a group whose pressure led Quraysh to drop the return clause.
- **Suhayl ibn Amr** — Quraysh's negotiator *(accepted Islam later)*.
- **Urwah ibn Mas'ud al-Thaqafi** — envoy who reported the Companions' love for the Prophet ﷺ *(accepted Islam later)*.
- **Budayl ibn Warqa' al-Khuza'i** — carried messages between the sides *(accepted Islam later)*.

### 26. The Conquest of Khaybar

In 7 AH, the Muslims besieged the fortresses of Khaybar. After days of failed attempts, the Prophet ﷺ said, "Tomorrow I will give the flag to a man who loves Allah and His Messenger and whom Allah and His Messenger love." Everyone hoped for it, but he called Ali, who had sore eyes. The Prophet ﷺ applied his saliva, the eyes were healed, and the fortress fell.

**Companions connected to this story**

- **Ali ibn Abi Talib** — given the flag; led the conquest of the fort.
- **Sahl ibn Sa'd** — narrated the hadith of the flag.
- **Muhammad ibn Maslamah** — killed the champion Marhab according to some reports (others name Ali).
- **Amir ibn al-Akwa'** — recited poetry on the march; died when his own sword rebounded, and the Prophet ﷺ affirmed his double reward.
- **Salamah ibn al-Akwa'** — his nephew, who narrated it.
- **Safiyyah bint Huyayy** — daughter of a Khaybar chief; married the Prophet ﷺ and became a Mother of the Believers.
- **Ja'far ibn Abi Talib** — returned from Abyssinia; the Prophet ﷺ said, "I do not know which makes me happier, the conquest of Khaybar or the return of Ja'far."
- **Abu Hurayrah** and **Abu Musa al-Ash'ari** — arrived around this time and joined the Muslims.
- **Bishr ibn al-Bara' ibn Ma'rur** — died after eating from the poisoned sheep offered to the Prophet ﷺ.

### 27. The Battle of Mu'tah

In 8 AH, 3,000 Muslims met a far larger Byzantine force. The Prophet ﷺ had named three commanders in turn: Zayd, then Ja'far, then Abdullah ibn Rawahah. All three were martyred. Khalid ibn al-Walid took the flag and withdrew the army skilfully. In Madinah, with tears in his eyes, the Prophet ﷺ announced each death before the news arrived, then said, "A sword from the swords of Allah took the flag."

**Companions connected to this story**

- **Zayd ibn Harithah** — first commander; martyred.
- **Ja'far ibn Abi Talib** — lost both hands holding the flag; given two wings in Paradise, hence "al-Tayyar."
- **Abdullah ibn Rawahah** — the poet; hesitated briefly, then advanced and was martyred.
- **Thabit ibn Aqram** — picked up the fallen flag and handed it to Khalid.
- **Khalid ibn al-Walid** — broke nine swords that day; named "Sword of Allah."
- **Abdullah ibn Umar** — counted over ninety wounds on Ja'far's front.
- **Asma bint Umays** — Ja'far's wife, to whom the Prophet ﷺ brought the news.
- **Abdullah ibn Ja'far** — Ja'far's son, whom the Prophet ﷺ took in his care.

### 28. The Conquest of Makkah

When Quraysh's allies broke the Hudaybiyah truce, the Prophet ﷺ marched with 10,000 men in Ramadan of 8 AH. Makkah fell almost without fighting. He entered with his head bowed in humility, cleared 360 idols from around the Ka'bah reciting "Truth has come and falsehood has vanished" (17:81), and told his former persecutors, "No blame on you today. Go, you are free."

**Companions connected to this story**

- **Hatib ibn Abi Balta'ah** — secretly wrote to Quraysh to protect his family; forgiven as a man of Badr.
- **Ali**, **al-Zubayr** and **al-Miqdad** — sent to intercept Hatib's letter from a woman on the road.
- **Umar ibn al-Khattab** — wanted to punish Hatib; told Allah may have forgiven the people of Badr.
- **Al-Abbas ibn Abd al-Muttalib** — brought Abu Sufyan to the Prophet ﷺ.
- **Abu Sufyan ibn Harb** — accepted Islam; "whoever enters Abu Sufyan's house is safe."
- **Sa'd ibn Ubadah** — said "Today is the day of slaughter"; the flag was given to his son **Qays ibn Sa'd**.
- **Khalid ibn al-Walid**, **al-Zubayr** and **Abu Ubaydah** — led columns into the city.
- **Bilal ibn Rabah** — called the adhan from the roof of the Ka'bah.
- **Uthman ibn Talhah** — the Prophet ﷺ returned the key of the Ka'bah to him, forever with his family.
- **Abu Quhafah** — Abu Bakr's elderly father; accepted Islam.
- **Umm Hani bint Abi Talib** — gave protection to two relatives, and the Prophet ﷺ honoured it.
- **Hind bint Utbah** — pledged allegiance among the women.
- **Ikrimah ibn Abi Jahl** and **Safwan ibn Umayyah** — fled, then returned and accepted Islam (see story 44).

### 29. The Battle of Hunayn

Weeks after the conquest, 12,000 Muslims met the Hawazin. Some were pleased by their numbers (9:25). Ambushed in a narrow valley, many fled, but the Prophet ﷺ rode forward saying, "I am the Prophet, no lie; I am the son of Abd al-Muttalib." Al-Abbas called the Companions back, and they won. When the spoils went to new Muslims of Quraysh, some Ansar felt hurt, and the Prophet ﷺ told them, "Are you not pleased that people go home with sheep and camels, and you go home with the Messenger of Allah?" They wept until their beards were wet.

**Companions connected to this story**

- **Al-Abbas ibn Abd al-Muttalib** — called out, "O people of the tree of Ridwan!"
- **Abu Sufyan ibn al-Harith** — the Prophet's cousin; held the reins of his mule.
- **Abu Bakr**, **Umar**, **Ali**, **al-Fadl ibn Abbas** and **Usamah ibn Zayd** — among the few who stood firm.
- **Ayman ibn Umm Ayman** — stood firm and was martyred.
- **Umm Sulaym** — carried a dagger to defend herself and the Prophet ﷺ.
- **Al-Bara' ibn Azib** — narrated the Prophet's words during the retreat.
- **Sa'd ibn Ubadah** — told the Prophet ﷺ of the Ansar's feelings and gathered them.
- **Abu Sa'id al-Khudri** — narrated the speech to the Ansar.
- **Al-Shayma' bint al-Harith** — his foster sister from Halimah, brought as a captive; he spread his cloak for her.
- **Malik ibn Awf al-Nasri** — commander of Hawazin *(accepted Islam later)*.

### 30. The Expedition of Tabuk

In 9 AH, in severe heat and poverty, the Prophet ﷺ called for an expedition to Tabuk against Byzantium, known as the "army of hardship." Companions gave all they could. Uthman equipped a large part of the army, and the Prophet ﷺ said, "Nothing Uthman does after today will harm him." The army waited twenty days at Tabuk, no battle took place, and the tribes of the north made treaties.

**Companions connected to this story**

- **Uthman ibn Affan** — gave hundreds of camels and 1,000 dinars.
- **Abu Bakr al-Siddiq** — gave all his wealth, leaving "Allah and His Messenger" for his family.
- **Umar ibn al-Khattab** — gave half his wealth, hoping to surpass Abu Bakr.
- **Abd al-Rahman ibn Awf** — gave a large sum.
- **Abu Aqil al-Ansari** — gave a small measure of dates earned by night labour; mocked by hypocrites, honoured by Allah (9:79).
- **Al-Bakka'un (the weepers)** — poor Companions with no mounts who wept at being left behind (9:92).
- **Ali ibn Abi Talib** — left in charge of the household: "Are you not pleased to be to me as Harun was to Musa?"
- **Abu Khaythamah** — stayed at first, then rode alone to catch up.
- **Abu Dharr al-Ghifari** — walked when his camel failed; "he walks alone, will die alone, and be raised alone."
- **Ka'b ibn Malik**, **Murarah ibn al-Rabi'** and **Hilal ibn Umayyah** — stayed behind (story 31).

### 31. Ka'b ibn Malik and the Three Who Stayed Behind

Ka'b had no excuse for missing Tabuk and refused to invent one. The Prophet ﷺ ordered the Muslims not to speak to him or his two companions for fifty days. The king of Ghassan wrote inviting him over; Ka'b threw the letter into an oven. On the fiftieth morning, Allah revealed their forgiveness (9:118), and a man ran up the mountain shouting the good news.

**Companions connected to this story**

- **Ka'b ibn Malik** — told the truth; narrated the whole story.
- **Murarah ibn al-Rabi'** and **Hilal ibn Umayyah** — the two other men of Badr who stayed behind.
- **Abu Qatadah al-Ansari** — Ka'b's cousin and closest friend, who would not reply to him.
- **Talhah ibn Ubaydillah** — rose and shook Ka'b's hand in the mosque; Ka'b never forgot it.
- **Hilal's wife** — allowed to keep serving her elderly husband.
- **Abdullah ibn Ka'b** — Ka'b's son, who narrated the story from him.
- **Abu Bakr al-Siddiq** — announced the good news from the mountain, according to some reports.

## Part Four: Character, Mercy and the Lives of the Companions

### 32. Uthman and the Well of Rumah

When the Muslims reached Madinah, sweet water was scarce, and the well of Rumah belonged to a man who sold its water at a high price. The Prophet ﷺ said, "Whoever buys the well of Rumah and makes it free for the Muslims will have a better one in Paradise." Uthman bought it, first half then all, and made it a free endowment for everyone.

**Companions connected to this story**

- **Uthman ibn Affan** — bought the well; reminded the people of it during the siege of his house years later.
- **The Muslims of Madinah** — who drank from it freely.

### 33. The Slander Against Aishah (al-Ifk)

Returning from an expedition, Aishah left the camp to search for a lost necklace, and the army departed without her. Safwan ibn al-Mu'attal found her and brought her back, leading the camel on foot. Hypocrites spread a lie about her, and for a month Madinah was troubled. Then Allah revealed ten verses of Surah al-Nur declaring her innocence.

**Companions connected to this story**

- **Aishah bint Abi Bakr** — declared innocent by revelation; narrated the full account.
- **Safwan ibn al-Mu'attal** — the Companion who found her; also cleared.
- **Abu Bakr al-Siddiq** and **Umm Ruman** — her parents, who suffered with her.
- **Barirah** — her servant, who testified she knew only good of her.
- **Zaynab bint Jahsh** — co-wife who, when asked, protected Aishah's honour.
- **Ali ibn Abi Talib** and **Usamah ibn Zayd** — consulted by the Prophet ﷺ.
- **Umm Mistah** — told Aishah of the rumours.
- **Mistah ibn Uthathah**, **Hassan ibn Thabit** and **Hamnah bint Jahsh** — repeated the slander and repented; Abu Bakr resumed supporting Mistah after verse 24:22.
- **Sa'd ibn Mu'adh** and **Sa'd ibn Ubadah** — argued in the mosque over how to deal with the slanderers.

### 34. Zayd ibn Harithah Chooses the Prophet ﷺ

Zayd was kidnapped as a child and sold in Makkah; Khadijah gave him to the Prophet ﷺ before prophethood. Years later his father and uncle found him and came to buy his freedom. The Prophet ﷺ let Zayd choose freely. Zayd chose to stay, saying he would choose no one over him. The Prophet ﷺ then declared him his son before Quraysh.

**Companions connected to this story**

- **Zayd ibn Harithah** — the only Companion named in the Qur'an (33:37).
- **Khadijah bint Khuwaylid** — gave Zayd to the Prophet ﷺ.
- **Usamah ibn Zayd** — his son, "the beloved son of the beloved."
- **Umm Ayman** — Zayd's later wife and Usamah's mother.

### 35. Abu Hurayrah and the Cup of Milk

Abu Hurayrah lived in the mosque with the poor of al-Suffah. One day, faint with hunger, he sat on the road hoping someone would invite him. The Prophet ﷺ saw his face, brought him home and sent him to call all the people of al-Suffah to share a single cup of milk. Abu Hurayrah served everyone first, then the Prophet ﷺ told him, "Drink," again and again, until he said, "I can drink no more." Abu Hurayrah also wept that his mother refused Islam. The Prophet ﷺ prayed for her, and he returned home to find her accepting Islam.

**Companions connected to this story**

- **Abu Hurayrah** — the narrator; later the most prolific narrator of hadith.
- **Umaymah bint Subayh** — his mother, who accepted Islam through the Prophet's prayer.
- **Ahl al-Suffah** — the poor Companions of the mosque who shared the cup.
- **Abu Bakr** and **Umar** — passed by first; Abu Hurayrah asked them about a verse hoping for food.

### 36. Abu Talhah and Umm Sulaym: Generosity and Patience

A hungry guest came to the Prophet ﷺ, and Abu Talhah took him home with only enough food for their children. Umm Sulaym put the children to sleep and dimmed the lamp so the guest would not see they were not eating. In the morning, the Prophet ﷺ said Allah had admired their deed, and verse 59:9 was revealed. When their young son later died, Umm Sulaym prepared her husband's meal before gently telling him, and the Prophet ﷺ prayed for blessing on their night. They were given a son, Abdullah, whose children all memorised the Qur'an.

**Companions connected to this story**

- **Abu Talhah al-Ansari (Zayd ibn Sahl)** — the host.
- **Umm Sulaym bint Milhan** — his wife; famous for her wisdom and patience. Her mahr was Abu Talhah's Islam.
- **Abu Umayr** — their young son who died; the Prophet ﷺ used to tease him about his pet bird.
- **Abdullah ibn Abi Talhah** — the son born after.
- **Anas ibn Malik** — Umm Sulaym's son, the narrator.
- **Abu Hurayrah** — narrated the hadith of the guest.

### 37. Sawad ibn Ghaziyyah at Badr

As the Prophet ﷺ straightened the ranks at Badr with an arrow, he poked Sawad in the stomach. Sawad said, "You have hurt me; let me take retribution." The Prophet ﷺ uncovered his stomach. Sawad embraced him and kissed it, saying, "I wanted my last contact in this world to be my skin touching yours." The Prophet ﷺ prayed for him.

**Companions connected to this story**

- **Sawad ibn Ghaziyyah** — the Companion of the ranks.
- **The army of Badr** — witnessed it as the ranks were straightened.

### 38. Julaybib

Julaybib was a poor man of humble appearance with no family. The Prophet ﷺ himself asked an Ansari family for their daughter's hand on his behalf. The parents hesitated, but the daughter said, "Will you reject the Messenger's request? Send me to him." Soon after, Julaybib was martyred in battle having killed seven. The Prophet ﷺ searched for him, laid him on his own arms, and said, "He is from me and I am from him."

**Companions connected to this story**

- **Julaybib** — the martyr.
- **His Ansari wife and her parents** — she is unnamed in the Sahih narration; the Prophet ﷺ prayed for abundant blessing on her.
- **Abu Barzah al-Aslami** — narrated the account in Sahih Muslim.

### 39. Mu'adh ibn Jabal Sent to Yemen

When sending Mu'adh to Yemen, the Prophet ﷺ asked how he would judge. Mu'adh said by the Book of Allah, then the Sunnah, then by his own reasoning. The Prophet ﷺ praised Allah. Another time he took Mu'adh's hand and said, "O Mu'adh, by Allah, I love you," and taught him to say after every prayer, "O Allah, help me to remember You, thank You and worship You well." Walking beside Mu'adh's mount as he left, he told him he might not see him again. Mu'adh wept.

**Companions connected to this story**

- **Mu'adh ibn Jabal** — the scholar of halal and haram.
- **Abu Musa al-Ash'ari** — sent to Yemen at the same time: "Make things easy, not difficult."

### 40. Abdullah ibn Umm Maktum and Surah Abasa

The Prophet ﷺ was inviting Quraysh leaders when the blind Companion Ibn Umm Maktum came asking to be taught. The Prophet ﷺ frowned and turned away, hoping to win the leaders. Allah revealed "He frowned and turned away" (80:1). Afterwards the Prophet ﷺ honoured him, saying, "Welcome to the one about whom my Lord reproached me," and made him deputy in Madinah many times.

**Companions connected to this story**

- **Abdullah ibn Umm Maktum** — the blind Companion; mu'adhdhin and deputy governor of Madinah.
- **Aishah** — narrated the occasion of revelation.

### 41. Usamah's Intercession and Justice for All

A woman of the noble Makhzum clan committed theft. Quraysh, worried for their status, asked Usamah, the Prophet's beloved, to intercede. The Prophet ﷺ said, "Do you intercede regarding a limit set by Allah?" He stood and declared that earlier nations were destroyed because they spared the noble and punished the weak, adding, "If Fatimah the daughter of Muhammad stole, I would cut her hand."

**Companions connected to this story**

- **Usamah ibn Zayd** — the intercessor, who asked forgiveness.
- **Fatimah bint al-Aswad al-Makhzumiyyah** — the woman; she repented and later lived righteously.
- **Aishah** — narrated the hadith and said the woman's repentance was sincere.
- **Fatimah bint Muhammad** — named as the example in his words.

### 42. Abu Dharr Accepts Islam

Hearing of a prophet in Makkah, Abu Dharr of Ghifar sent his brother, then went himself. He sat in the mosque for days, afraid to ask. Ali noticed the stranger and quietly led him to the Prophet ﷺ, where he accepted Islam. Abu Dharr then shouted the testimony of faith at the Ka'bah and was beaten until al-Abbas warned Quraysh that Ghifar lay on their trade route. He returned home, and half his tribe accepted Islam.

**Companions connected to this story**

- **Abu Dharr al-Ghifari (Jundub ibn Junadah)** — the seeker.
- **Unays** — his brother, who brought the first report.
- **Ali ibn Abi Talib** — hosted him and guided him to the Prophet ﷺ.
- **Al-Abbas ibn Abd al-Muttalib** — rescued him from the beating.
- **Abdullah ibn Abbas** — narrated the story.
- **Tribes of Ghifar and Aslam** — accepted Islam through him.

### 43. Al-Tufayl ibn Amr al-Dawsi

Al-Tufayl, a poet and chief of Daws, was warned by Quraysh not to listen to the Prophet ﷺ, so he stuffed cotton in his ears. Then he thought, "I am an intelligent man; I can tell good from bad." He listened, accepted Islam, and asked for a sign. A light shone on his whip as he returned. When his tribe was slow to respond, the Prophet ﷺ prayed, "O Allah, guide Daws," and later eighty families came to Madinah.

**Companions connected to this story**

- **Al-Tufayl ibn Amr al-Dawsi** — the chief; later martyred at Yamamah.
- **Abu Hurayrah** — of Daws; accepted Islam through al-Tufayl.
- **Al-Tufayl's father and wife** — accepted Islam when he returned.

### 44. Ikrimah ibn Abi Jahl's Return

After the conquest of Makkah, Ikrimah, son of the Prophet's fiercest enemy, fled to Yemen. His wife Umm Hakim accepted Islam and secured a pardon for him. When he arrived, the Prophet ﷺ stood and greeted him warmly, and told his Companions not to insult Ikrimah's father in front of him, for insulting the dead hurts the living. Ikrimah became a devoted Muslim and was martyred at Yarmuk.

**Companions connected to this story**

- **Ikrimah ibn Abi Jahl** — the one who returned.
- **Umm Hakim bint al-Harith** — his wife, who brought him back.
- **Safwan ibn Umayyah** — also fled and was pardoned through **Umayr ibn Wahb**.

### 45. Wahshi, the Killer of Hamzah

Wahshi, an Abyssinian slave, killed Hamzah at Uhud in return for his freedom. After the conquest of Makkah he feared for his life, then learned the Prophet ﷺ did not kill those who came to Islam. He came and accepted Islam. The Prophet ﷺ forgave him but asked, "Can you keep your face away from me?" Years later, Wahshi killed the false prophet Musaylimah at Yamamah with the same spear.

**Companions connected to this story**

- **Wahshi ibn Harb** — accepted Islam and sought to atone.
- **Hamzah ibn Abd al-Muttalib** — the beloved uncle he had killed.
- **Jubayr ibn Mut'im** — his master, who promised his freedom *(accepted Islam later)*.
- **Hind bint Utbah** — urged him on at Uhud *(accepted Islam later)*.

### 46. Thumamah ibn Uthal

Thumamah, a chief of Yamamah, was captured by a Muslim cavalry unit and tied to a pillar of the mosque. For three days the Prophet ﷺ asked him how he was and treated him kindly, then freed him without ransom. Thumamah bathed, returned and accepted Islam, saying, "No face on earth was more hateful to me than yours; now it is the most beloved." He then cut off grain from Yamamah to Makkah until Quraysh begged the Prophet ﷺ to intervene.

**Companions connected to this story**

- **Thumamah ibn Uthal** — the chief.
- **Muhammad ibn Maslamah** — led the cavalry unit that captured him, according to reports.
- **Abu Hurayrah** — narrated the story in al-Bukhari and Muslim.

### 47. The Delegation of Najran and the Mubahalah

A Christian delegation from Najran came to discuss Isa (Jesus). When they would not accept the Qur'an's teaching, Allah commanded the Prophet ﷺ to invite them to a mutual invocation of Allah's curse on the liars (3:61). He came out with Ali, Fatimah, Hasan and Husayn. The delegation declined and made a peace treaty instead. They asked for a trustworthy man, and he sent Abu Ubaydah, "the trustworthy one of this Ummah."

**Companions connected to this story**

- **Ali ibn Abi Talib**, **Fatimah bint Muhammad**, **al-Hasan** and **al-Husayn** — brought out for the Mubahalah.
- **Abu Ubaydah ibn al-Jarrah** — sent with them as "Amin al-Ummah."
- **Sa'd ibn Abi Waqqas** — narrated the account in Sahih Muslim.
- **Hudhayfah ibn al-Yaman** — narrated the hadith of the trustworthy man.

### 48. Ali and Fatimah: Marriage and the Gift of Tasbih

Ali married Fatimah with a simple mahr from the sale of his armour. Their home was humble. Fatimah's hands were blistered from the grinding stone, and Ali's chest ached from drawing water, so they asked the Prophet ﷺ for a servant from the captives. He came to them at night and said, "Shall I tell you something better than a servant? When you go to bed, say SubhanAllah 33 times, Alhamdulillah 33 times and Allahu Akbar 34 times." Ali said he never left it afterwards.

**Companions connected to this story**

- **Ali ibn Abi Talib** — narrated the hadith.
- **Fatimah bint Muhammad** — "leader of the women of Paradise."
- **Aishah** — Fatimah first came to her house and found the Prophet ﷺ absent.
- **Uthman ibn Affan** — bought Ali's armour, according to some reports.
- **Asma bint Umays** and **Umm Ayman** — helped prepare the wedding, according to reports.

### 49. The Farewell Pilgrimage

In 10 AH, the Prophet ﷺ made his only Hajj with over 100,000 people. At Arafah he gave his great sermon: lives, wealth and honour are sacred; usury and blood feuds of ignorance are abolished; treat women well; hold to the Book of Allah. He asked, "Have I conveyed?" and they said, "Yes," and he raised his finger to the sky: "O Allah, bear witness." Then the verse was revealed: "This day I have perfected your religion for you" (5:3).

**Companions connected to this story**

- **Jabir ibn Abdullah** — narrated the longest, most detailed description of the Hajj.
- **Rabi'ah ibn Umayyah ibn Khalaf** — repeated the sermon loudly so the crowd could hear.
- **Usamah ibn Zayd** — rode behind the Prophet ﷺ from Arafah to Muzdalifah.
- **Al-Fadl ibn Abbas** — rode behind him from Muzdalifah to Mina.
- **Ali ibn Abi Talib** — came from Yemen and joined him with sacrificial animals.
- **Aishah** — her menstruation began; the Prophet ﷺ comforted her and she did Umrah after.
- **Asma bint Umays** — gave birth to Muhammad ibn Abi Bakr at Dhul-Hulayfah.
- **Umar ibn al-Khattab** — later confirmed the day and place verse 5:3 was revealed.
- **Bilal ibn Rabah** — shaded the Prophet ﷺ with a cloth during the stoning, by some reports.
- **Abu Bakr**, **Uthman**, all of the Mothers of the Believers, and the great mass of Companions — performed Hajj with him.

### 50. The Final Illness and Passing of the Prophet ﷺ

In Rabi' al-Awwal of 11 AH, the Prophet ﷺ fell ill and asked to be nursed in Aishah's house. When he could no longer lead the prayer, he said, "Tell Abu Bakr to lead the people." On his last morning he lifted the curtain and smiled at the rows behind Abu Bakr. He died with his head on Aishah's chest, saying, "With the highest companion." When Umar refused to believe it, Abu Bakr recited, "Muhammad is but a messenger; messengers have passed away before him" (3:144), and said, "Whoever worshipped Muhammad, Muhammad has died; whoever worshipped Allah, Allah is alive and never dies."

**Companions connected to this story**

- **Aishah** — he died in her house, with his head on her chest.
- **Abu Bakr al-Siddiq** — led the prayers; gave the address that steadied the Ummah; became the first Caliph.
- **Umar ibn al-Khattab** — overcome by grief, then accepted it when he heard the verse.
- **Fatimah bint Muhammad** — he whispered she would be the first of his family to join him; she wept, then smiled.
- **Abd al-Rahman ibn Abi Bakr** — brought the siwak the Prophet ﷺ used in his last moments.
- **Ali**, **al-Abbas**, **al-Fadl** and **Qutham ibn Abbas**, **Usamah ibn Zayd** and **Shuqran** — washed his blessed body.
- **Abu Talhah al-Ansari** — dug his grave.
- **Usamah ibn Zayd** — commander of the army he had ordered to Syria; Abu Bakr sent it after his death.
- **Anas ibn Malik** — said, "The day he came to Madinah everything shone; the day he died everything darkened."
- **Bilal ibn Rabah** — could not finish the adhan for grief, and soon left Madinah.
- **Umm Ayman** — wept that revelation from heaven had ceased.
- **Abdullah ibn Abbas** — narrated many details of the final days.

## A Note on Sources

These stories were written from general knowledge of the classical sources, without live access to them, so names, numbers and attributions should be checked before teaching or publishing. Where reports differ, the stories note "according to some reports."

The main sources for these accounts are Sahih al-Bukhari and Sahih Muslim for the hadith narrations; the Sirah of Ibn Ishaq as edited by Ibn Hisham; al-Tabaqat al-Kubra of Ibn Sa'd and al-Isabah of Ibn Hajar for Companion biographies; and modern works such as *Al-Raheeq al-Makhtum* (The Sealed Nectar) by Safiur Rahman Mubarakpuri. A local scholar can confirm details and gradings of narrations.
