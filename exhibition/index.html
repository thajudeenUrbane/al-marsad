Why al-Marṣad

لماذا المرصد؟

The Sīra of the Prophet ﷺ (peace and blessings be upon him) is reported across many books, by many narrators.

A reader cannot easily see where each statement comes from, how it was carried, or where reports differ.

al-Marṣad shows where each statement comes from, and leaves every ruling to scholars.

Where it comes from

A statement from Ibn Hishām’s Sīra carries its volume and page, and its line in the digital text.

How it was carried

Where the text gives a chain of narrators, it is recorded link by link: chains so far.

Where reports differ

Each report is kept with its own source. The software never chooses between them.

What al-Marṣad will not do

  • Depict the Prophet ﷺ, his family, the Companions, or any person.
  • Give a ruling or a fatwa.
  • Let software grade the authenticity of a report.
  • Show a contested matter as settled.
  • Close itself off: everything stays open to check, copy and correct.

How it is made: one maintainer, with AI help. The statements were drawn from the text with AI help, and every one stays “proposed” until a scholar accepts it.

Say

The problem in one breath: many books, many narrators, and the reader cannot see the ground under a statement. al-Marṣad shows the ground and stops there: the ruling is the scholar’s.

Say the AI part plainly here, in the first minutes: AI helped draw the statements from the text and wrote the short English glosses. Nothing is accepted until a scholar accepts it.

If asked

Is AI writing the history?
No. AI drafts and the software proposes; every statement is marked “proposed”. A scholar accepts, rejects or sends it for discussion. Software never accepts a claim on its own.
Which sources?
Today one book: Ibn Hishām’s Sīra, Cairo 1955 edition, with page numbers still provisional (not yet checked against print). The digital text is OpenITI’s. Which source comes next is for the scholars to choose.
Is this not what the takhrīj tools already do?
They do much of it for ḥadīth, and well. al-Marṣad is narrower and different: the Sīra as one connected record of people, events, places and reports, open for scholars to rule on, one statement at a time.

If asked

Why are some people drawn dark?
The Atlas draws a person dark when a statement records the person as fighting against or opposing the Prophet ﷺ. Who classifies opponents and conversions, and how to show Abū Ṭālib, the munāfiqūn and the Jewish clans of Madinah, is one of the questions we bring to him today.
Why this order?
The Atlas puts the Prophet ﷺ first, then the four caliphs, then the ten promised Paradise, then the rest by recorded links, that is, by how often the sources mention them: coverage, never rank. People recorded as opposing him come last.
A report label in the side panel?
Some reports carry a software description of whom the words are ascribed to. It is a description, not a grading, and whether to show it at all is open. The scholar’s term is his to give.
Is this the whole Sīra?
It is Ibn Hishām’s Sīra only, as one book tells it.

If the view stays dark for more than a few seconds, press S for the still, and carry on.

If asked

Is this report mursal?
That is his determination, not ours. Abū Jaʿfar is a Successor; the record keeps the chain as the text gives it and leaves its grading to a scholar. If the Atlas shows a software label on this report, it is a description of the ascription, not a grading, and we would value his view on whether to show it at all.
Why no Arabic text of the whole report on screen?
The digital text is licensed for sharing with conditions (CC BY-NC-SA). We keep pointers and short phrases, and we have written to OpenITI about permission and partnership.
Why “provisional” beside the edition?
The page numbers come from the digital text and have not yet been checked against the printed Cairo edition.

What is built, what waits

ما بُني وما ينتظر

statements reviewed by a scholar so far.

Built

  • marks in the sky: name records, events, places, stories, groups.
  • statements, every one proposed. of them are lines of descent.
  • chains of narration recorded link by link.
  • Expeditions: Badr, Uḥud, the Hijra, ʿAqaba and Abyssinia, each with a halaqa room.
  • A review queue: statements in batches.
  • A pilot pack, ready to send: statements and the first identity questions.

Waits

  • identity questions (“is this the same person?”) and proposed merges of name records.
  • of events have a date; of places have a location.
  • A review board, and the tradition it declares.
  • A scholar’s first decision.

If asked

What is in the pilot pack?
statements in short batches, from the 50 Stories. An AI wrote those stories and nobody knows yet whether they are accurate, so each statement needs a real source with its page before it can be accepted. Then “same person?” questions. The reviewer uses a phone or a laptop and sends one small file back.
Why so few dates and places?
Dates and places are filled only where the text gives them and the extraction caught them. That is why we keep Map and Time off today.
Lines of descent?
“So-and-so, son of so-and-so”: the names carry the lineage, and each link is a statement waiting like the rest.
