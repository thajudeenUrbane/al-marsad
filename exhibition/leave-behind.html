A map of the Sīra of the Prophet ﷺ (peace and blessings be upon him): it shows where each report comes from, and leaves every ruling to scholars.

What it is

What it does

  • Where it comes from. A statement from the Sīra of Ibn Hishām رحمه الله carries its volume and page, and its line in the digital text.
  • How it was carried. Where the text gives a chain of narrators, it is recorded link by link.
  • Where reports differ. Each report is kept with its own source. The software never chooses between them.

What it will not do

  • Depict the Prophet ﷺ, his family, the Companions, or any person.
  • Give a ruling or a fatwa.
  • Let software grade the authenticity of a report.
  • Show a contested matter as settled.
  • Close itself off: everything stays open to check, copy and correct.

How it is made. One maintainer compiled the statements with AI help. The 50 Stories were written by AI and are not a source, so each claim taken from them needs a real source. Every statement stays “proposed” until a scholar accepts it.

What is built, what waits ما بُني وما ينتظر

statements reviewed by a scholar so far. All statements are proposed.

Built

  • A sky of marks: name records, events, places, stories, groups.
  • chains of narration recorded link by link.
  • Expeditions: Badr, Uḥud, the Hijra, ʿAqaba and Abyssinia, each with a halaqa room.
  • A review queue: statements in batches.
  • A pilot pack, ready to send: statements from the 50 Stories, each needing a real source before it can be accepted, and the first identity questions.

Waits

  • identity questions (“is this the same person?”) and proposed merges of name records.
  • of events have a date; of places have a location.
  • A review board, and the tradition it declares.
  • A scholar’s first decision.
