# Funding options: draft

Realistic routes for year one and beyond, for the maintainer to weigh. Budget to raise: **$200k–335k (full) or $80k–125k (lean)** in year one (see `lab/beyond-the-graph`, "Team and cost"). Anything marked **to check** has not been verified; no grant programme's details below are confirmed.

**Ground rules for every route** (maintainer decisions)
- Non-profit and donation-funded; everything we make stays free and open (code AGPL, data CC BY-SA, identifiers CC0).
- Quarterly totals by category, published (M3). Spending as in M2.
- No dark patterns: no fake urgency or countdowns, no guilt copy, no pre-ticked recurring gifts, easy cancellation.
- No funder buys editorial control. Scholars decide content; a gift never changes a claim, a verdict or what is shown.
- Many routes need a legal home. The trust's country is decided later, after consultation (M1), and this is the main blocker for routes 3–5.

## 1. Individual donors
- **Fit:** Strongest. A clear, sourced, non-profit cause appeals to people who care about the Sīra and about accuracy. Sefaria and BibleProject show that open, free, donor-funded religious education can reach scale (BibleProject reports about 48,000 donors in 2023).
- **Effort:** Personal conversations, the one-pager, a short demo video, a simple donation page, thank-you notes, quarterly reports.
- **Timing:** Start now with 20–30 people the maintainer knows; aim first for the lean plan.
- **Risks:** Too dependent on one person; donors from one circle may expect one viewpoint. Mitigation: publish the rules above before taking money.
- **To check:** Which payment platform suits a non-profit before the trust exists, and tax treatment for donors in each country.

## 2. Ramadan and Rabīʿ al-Awwal campaigns
- **Fit:** High. Giving and Qurʾān and Sīra use rise sharply in Ramadan (Quran Foundation reports usage doubling). Rabīʿ al-Awwal is the natural season for the Sīra.
- **Effort:** High for a few weeks: a launch moment (for example, the Hijrah World opening to the public), short daily posts from the sources, a progress page that tells the truth.
- **Timing:** Plan four months ahead. Next Ramadan begins around early February 2027 (**to check** exact dates).
- **Risks:** Crowded season; pressure to use urgency tactics. Mitigation: a calm, factual campaign with a real deliverable, no countdown clocks.
- **Zakat:** Do not accept zakat unless scholars confirm the project is an eligible category and the funds can be kept separate (**to check** with the scholars; default: sadaqa only).

## 3. Waqf or endowment
- **Fit:** High for the long term. A perpetual waqf for Sīra scholarship has deep precedent, and running costs are low (hosting under $600 a year), so a modest endowment could keep the platform alive indefinitely.
- **Effort:** High: legal structure, trustees, an investment policy that is Sharīʿa-compliant, and a scholar on the board.
- **Timing:** After the trust's country is chosen (M1). Possibly a "maintenance waqf" target first: enough to cover hosting, domain and a small stipend for ever.
- **Risks:** Set-up and compliance costs; locking in governance too early. Cash waqf rules differ by country (**to check**).
- **To check:** Whether existing waqf administrators or platforms will hold a waqf for an open digital project.

## 4. Islamic philanthropy foundations
- **Fit:** Medium to high. Many foundations fund Islamic education, Qurʾān and heritage work; fewer fund open digital infrastructure.
- **Effort:** Medium–high: research, introductions, proposals, reports.
- **Timing:** 6–12 months from first contact.
- **Risks:** A foundation's narrative, sectarian or political expectations; requests for exclusivity or branding over content. Mitigation: the ground rules above, in the agreement.
- **To check:** Which foundations currently fund digital or open-source Islamic education, their eligibility rules (many require a registered charity), and deadlines. Build a list from their own websites, not from memory.

## 5. Digital-humanities and education grants
- **Fit:** Medium. The project is strong digital humanities (open data, provenance, text reuse with OpenITI and KITAB, published learning trials). Some funders exclude religious instruction; frame the work as historical sources, open data and learning science.
- **Effort:** High: formal applications, usually with a university partner as lead or co-applicant.
- **Timing:** 6–18 months; fixed calls.
- **Risks:** Low success rates; reporting burden; scope drift towards the funder's priorities.
- **Best route:** Through the learning-science partner (letter 7) or OpenITI/KITAB (letter 1), applying jointly. **To check:** Current calls from national research councils and private foundations in the partners' countries.

## 6. Matching campaigns and community "pay it forward"
- **Fit:** Medium–high once there is something public to show. The TV series *The Chosen* is often cited for funding through its audience: early crowdfunding, then viewers giving so that others can watch free (**to check** the details and current structure before citing it publicly). The lesson for us: let people fund free access for others.
- **Model for us:** One or more major donors agree to match gifts up to a stated amount; the campaign funds a specific, visible thing ("the Hijrah World in Urdu and Malayalam", "scholar review of the Badr journey").
- **Effort:** Medium: find the matcher first, then a clear page with honest progress.
- **Risks:** Fake or soft matches are a dark pattern; never claim a match that is not committed in writing. Unmet targets: say what happens to the money beforehand.

## 7. In-kind and partner support
- **Fit:** Medium. Non-profit cloud credits, design or translation volunteered by professionals, academic time through partners, and API access (Quran Foundation).
- **Effort:** Low–medium.
- **Risks:** Hidden dependence on a free tier that can end; volunteers drifting away. Mitigation: keep the core static and portable; pay stipends for scholar review rather than relying on goodwill.
- **To check:** Current non-profit credit programmes of hosting and AI providers, and their eligibility.

## Suggested order
1. **Now:** individual donors in the maintainer's network, aiming for the lean plan ($80k–125k); set up the donation page with the ground rules; in-kind support.
2. **Before next Ramadan:** a public Hijrah World and one honest campaign, ideally with a committed match.
3. **Within the year:** one joint grant application with an academic partner; conversations with two or three foundations.
4. **After M1:** the trust, then a maintenance waqf.
