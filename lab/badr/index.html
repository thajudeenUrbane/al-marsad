Badr: stand there, then ask how we know

Two rooms. In the first you stand in the valley of Badr under the sky that astronomy computes for the eve of the battle. In the second you investigate a real question in the oldest surviving full biography: why its list of names and its own total do not agree.

A prototype, built to test ideas. Nothing here is scholar-reviewed. The names are claims an AI extraction pass proposed from Ibn Hishām's Sīra. The terrain is today's ground. The sky is a calculation. Press Provenance lens at any time to see which is which.

Room 2

The 314: an investigation

Ibn Isḥāq (d. about 150 AH) names everyone he counted at Badr, clan by clan, and closes with totals. His student's student, Ibn Hishām (d. 213 or 218 AH), edited the book and added notes of his own. You will do what a reviewer does: compare a list with what it claims.

1Do the totals agree with each other?

Ibn Isḥāq gives three totals: 83 Emigrants, 61 of the Aws, 170 of the Khazraj. Then a grand total: 314.

Before anything else: add the three. Does the author's own arithmetic hold?

2Predict before you look

The graph read the whole list and found 321 different names: more than 314.

Why might a list hold more names than its own total? Choose every reason you think is possible. There is no penalty for guessing; predicting first is how this sticks.

All five are real possibilities, and historians check for each one. Now look at the evidence.

3Look at every name

Each dot is one name the list holds, grouped as the text groups them. Colour says whose voice the name is in. A ring means the text says the person was given a share without being present. Open any dot to see its claim.

4Now the gap runs the other way

Take away Ibn Hishām's additions and the list is now short of 314. So there are two problems at once. Which layer could each one live in?

The last answer is the honest one. The totals agree with each other, so the author's arithmetic holds. The extra names come mostly from the editor's notes, which the text marks. The shortfall is most likely in our own machine reading, which tags voice imperfectly and may have missed names. Only a reviewer with the Arabic passage beside them can settle each one.

That is why this mismatch sits in al-Marṣad's review queue as badr_all: MISMATCH, to review, and why the graph never "corrects" the source to make the numbers fit.

Three layers can disagree: what happened, the text that reports it, and our reading of the text. Good history keeps them apart. You just did.

5In your own words

Explain to a friend, in two sentences, why "314 at Badr" and "321 names in the list" are both true. It stays on this device.
