The verdict

What went wrong, and the one change that fixes it

We built an excellent evidence engine and then showed people the engine. A ring of names around one person, a yellow "not reviewed" banner and a table of citations serve an auditor. A learner cannot use them. The data is not the problem: 15,019 sourced relations from 1,788 sections of Ibn Hishām is rare material. The problem is that nothing turns it into an experience.

The graph becomes the engine. The product becomes a world: places you stand in, a story you are guided through, a companion that asks before it tells, and a sky that remembers what you have learned.

Enter

Real terrain of the Ḥijāz at night. The Prophet ﷺ is the light, people are lights, places appear only where a gazetteer locates them, and gaps are shown as gaps. Nothing is depicted.

Be guided

Chapters built from scenes. A scene is a place, a moment, people and sourced claims, reviewed as one unit, so scholars review what learners actually see.

Remember

Guess first, then read. Recall at the end of each chapter, spaced reviews, and a personal sky of everyone met. These are the methods with the strongest evidence in learning science.

Ask

A companion that cannot assert by construction. The model chooses claim IDs and teaching moves; the claims are shown verbatim from the graph; questions it cannot answer are referred to a scholar.

Prove it

Measure delayed learning, at one week and one month, from the first pilot onward. Almost no immersive heritage project has published this; it would set us apart.

What the prototype showed

Hijrah Night: built today, from committed files only, in about 1,000 lines

To test the central bet before planning around it, I built a working slice: ten chapters of the Hijrah over real terrain (about 1 km per cell), using 277 claims from 28 sections of the full-book Ibn Hishām pass. Every sentence a learner reads is a claim's gloss with its volume and page. The only text written by hand is chapter titles, camera positions and questions, and the questions assert nothing.

What it proved

  1. The data can carry an arc on its own. Sections 659–672 record who emigrated first and who lodged them at Qubāʾ and al-Sunḥ, so the first chapter gathers the lights of 39 people around Qubāʾ while the Prophet ﷺ is still in Makkah. In chapter 9 his light arrives where they are waiting. Nobody wrote that arc; the claims contain it.
  2. Questions can be generated entirely from claims, including the wrong answers. "Who stayed behind in Makkah?" Abū Bakr and ʿAlī are right (§678). ʿUmar is wrong because §665 records his emigration to Madinah, and the learner is shown that claim. Distractors with evidence teach as much as the answers do.
  3. Disagreement is a lesson, not a defect. §698 gives two reports of where the Prophet ﷺ lodged at Qubāʾ (Kulthūm b. Hidm or Saʿd b. Khaythama). The question accepts both and explains why. The arrival date also has two claims ("Monday, 12 Rabīʿ al-Awwal" and "twelve nights gone").
  4. Honest gaps look intentional, not broken. Five of the eleven route stops (Amaj, Qudayd, Marjiḥ, Dhū Kashr, Dhū Salam) have no reviewed location, so they appear as dashed stops and the light goes dark between ʿUsfān and al-ʿArj. No line is drawn between stops, because the text gives their order, not the road.
  5. The isnād can be seen. In Study depth each section's chain rises from the scene toward the book: Ibn Isḥāq, al-Zuhrī, ʿAbd al-Raḥmān b. Mālik, his father, Surāqa. Provenance becomes something you can look at.

What it exposed

  1. The graph records relations, not what happened. Section 680, the night of leaving, produced exactly one story claim: Abū Jahl was at the door. ʿAlī sleeping in the bed and the watchers being blinded exist only in an AI-written section heading. About 61% of the full pass is "father of". We need a layer of moments (who did what, where, in what order), or every world will feel like a genealogy.
  2. Geography is the bottleneck for immersion. Of 236 places in the graph, 10 have a coordinate matched with exact or probable confidence, and none is reviewed. The prototype had to match al-Thurayya by name for ʿUsfān, al-ʿArj and Qubāʾ.
  3. Identity problems show up in the experience. The guide appears as ʿAbd Allāh b. Urayqiṭ (§691) and ʿAbd Allāh b. Arqaṭ (§696), so a learner's sky holds two stars for one man. Merge resolution (round 3, F3) is learner-facing, not only bookkeeping.

Each one shaped the current product. The evidence says to change it.

What the world has tried

The strongest examples and failures from five research reviews, each with the lesson for us. Figures are as the sources report them; vendor claims are marked.

Immersive history

Assassin's Creed Discovery Tour

Ubisoft, 2018 onward; "History of Baghdad" with the Institut du monde arabe, 2023
About 330 students: the tour raised knowledge 22–44%; teacher-led classes gained more. Tested immediately only.
LessonA beautiful world without a task loses to a teacher. Baghdad's content unlocks by discovery, which is worth copying.

Google Expeditions

Google, closed June 2021
Closed because schools lacked headsets and hybrid learning made it hard to use. Tours moved to web and phone.
LessonHardware dependence kills reach. Web first; headsets are an extra.

USC Shoah Foundation, Dimensions in Testimony

12+ museum installations
Visitors ask questions; the system plays only answers survivors actually recorded, and asks people to rephrase when nothing matches.
LessonThe closest model to our rule: answer only with recorded words, never generated ones.

Venice Time Machine

EPFL and the Venice State Archive, suspended 2019
A non-binding agreement with no data licensing; the archive called 8 TB of scans unusable because provenance metadata did not meet archival standards (Nature).
LessonLicences, governance and provenance decide survival. Our provenance discipline is an asset; keep it.

Never Alone

Upper One Games with about 40 Iñupiat elders
24 short films of community voices unlock as you play.
LessonCo-design with the tradition's own authorities gives legitimacy. Our scholars should shape scenes, not only check claims.

Lighthouse "Immersive Van Gogh"; NFB Interactive

Bankrupt 2023; studios closed January 2024
7M+ tickets and still bankrupt; one-off interactive documentaries cost double their budgets.
LessonSpectacle is fragile. Build one reusable engine that produces many worlds from data.
AI tutors and companions

Harvard physics AI tutor

Kestin et al., Scientific Reports, 2025; 194 students
More than twice the learning gain of an active-learning class (0.63 SD). The tutor was given pre-written step-by-step solutions, and the platform, not the prompt, enforced the sequence.
LessonA fixed answer key plus enforced steps. For us, the claims are the answer key.

Khanmigo

NBER working paper, August 2026; 18 schools, two years
0.06–0.08 SD per year, no better than Khan Academy practice without AI. The median student messaged it on about a third of practice days.
LessonA tutor nobody talks to teaches nothing. Ours has to start the conversation, and teachers have to bring it into class.

Unguarded vs guarded AI

Bastani et al., PNAS 2025; about 1,000 students
Unguarded ChatGPT raised practice scores 48% but left students 17% worse once it was removed; a guarded tutor did no harm.
LessonRecall before reveal, always. Even a quotation comes after the learner has tried.

Character.AI, Meta, "Father Justin"

2023–2025
User-made "Muhammad" and "Jesus" bots removed by Meta after press reports; Character.AI ended open chat for under-18s; an AI priest was demoted after offering confession.
LessonNever a persona of the Prophet ﷺ or the Companions. The companion speaks as itself, labelled as AI.

Usul.ai and Ansari

Non-profit and open-source Islamic AI
Retrieval over OpenITI and Shamela with inspectable citations; Usul states it is "not for fatwas".
LessonOur nearest technical neighbours, and potential partners. Our difference is a reviewed, structured graph and a learning design.

Faithful citations

arXiv 2412.18004 and a 2026 replication
Up to 57% of RAG citations were added after the fact in adversarial tests; about 1 in 7 in a 2026 replication.
LessonNever let a model write a sentence and then cite it. It chooses claims; the page shows them.
Faith education and the Sīra

Sefaria

Open library of Jewish texts, non-profit
1M+ monthly users (2026); 584,000 user-made source sheets (2024); an AI Library Assistant designed to send readers to sources.
LessonOur closest model: open texts, a linked graph, user-made sheets, AI that points to sources. Copy the source sheet as a "majlis sheet".

BibleProject

Donor-funded, everything free
620M+ views; FY2025 revenue $30.9M; 48,000 donors in 2023.
LessonCraft and theme-led storytelling attract donors at scale. A donor-funded project can afford a world-class designer.

Quran.com / Quran Foundation

Non-profit with open APIs
63M+ active users on the web in a year; usage doubles in Ramadan.
LessonPlan launches around the calendar: Ramadan and Rabīʿ al-Awwal. Link occasions of revelation through their API.

Prophet's Biography Museum, Madinah

Muslim World League, 2021
25+ pavilions in 7 languages; the Rabat branch reports 3M visitors. No online corpus, no footnotes.
LessonThe institutional benchmark for presentation without depiction. Our edge is a citation behind every statement, available anywhere.

Ithra, "Hijrah: In the Footsteps of the Prophet"

2022–23, then the National Museum, Riyadh
14 zones, route imagery, calligraphy and manuscripts; 100,000+ visitors.
LessonThe closest precedent to Hijrah Night. Their route research is a partnership opportunity.

The Hijrah trail ("ʿAlā Khuṭāh")

Saudi General Entertainment Authority, inaugurated about February 2026
About 470 km from Thawr to Qubāʾ, 41 restored landmarks, a target of 5M visitors a year by 2030.
LessonMillions will walk the route with no sourced companion. A place-anchored, cited audio guide is a large opening (X6).

Patterns that keep working

Learning ends in retrieval, not in a "next" button. Real places act as memory anchors. Real voices and source texts carry emotion and authority. Discovery unlocks content. Short immersive segments are followed by reflection. Teachers and groups multiply reach. Open data outlives the apps built on it.

Patterns that keep failing

Hardware-gated products. Spectacle with no task. Generated speech presented as a person. Leaderboards and compulsory badges. Success measured by engagement or completion (MOOC completion fell to about 3%). One-off bespoke formats. Ambitious projects without licensing and governance.

What nobody has done yet

An immersive world in which every rendered thing resolves to a source passage. Disagreement between reports shown side by side as a learning event. A generative tutor that cannot assert by construction. Knowledge tracing over a historical claim graph rather than maths skills. A randomised trial of AI tutoring in history or religious studies (every trial found was in maths, physics or English). A codified way to present a sacred history immersively without depiction. All six are within our reach.

The experience we should build

Six layers people touch, one engine underneath

WorldSeventh-century Arabia as real terrain you can enter, at the time of day and season the text supports. Places appear only when located, with a certainty grade. Time can be scrubbed, and on the Hijri calendar the world follows the year: on 12 Rabīʿ al-Awwal the light arrives at Qubāʾ.
JourneysGuided chapters made of scenes: the Hijrah, the people of Badr, the first believers, then every documented event (round 3, E6). Each scene has a place, a moment, people, claims, a camera, a question and an approval.
InvestigationsQuests that are real historical questions. When did they reach Qubāʾ? With whom did he lodge? Why the coastal route? Learners weigh reports, trace chains to their eyewitness, and record their reasoning.
CompanionA guide that asks before it tells, proposes the next path, turns a question into a query that moves the camera, shows claims verbatim, and refers rulings to a scholar. It speaks as itself, never as a person of the Sīra.
SkyYour memory, made visible. Everyone you meet becomes a star, each journey a constellation; stars brighten as you recall them. Spaced reviews bring you back; nothing ranks you against anyone else.
CircleThe halaqa, the classroom and the family. A teacher drives the camera on every phone in the room, runs guess-first questions live, and hands out majlis sheets (claims gathered for a lesson). Families get "teach it back" prompts.
EngineOne graph: claims, moments, places, scenes, reviews. Compiled into static packs (scenes, terrain tiles, audio, question banks, indexes) served almost free. The research depth keeps full access to the data.

Three people, one graph

Maryam, 13, in Kochi, opens the Hijrah on a mid-range phone. She guesses who stayed behind in Makkah, gets ʿUmar wrong, and sees why: §665 has him emigrating earlier. Two days later her sky shows five stars due for review; she remembers four. In class her teacher projects the cave at Thawr and the whole room answers the same question at once.

Yusuf, a teacher in Birmingham, builds a majlis sheet for Friday: the two reports of the lodging at Qubāʾ, the chain behind each, and one investigation. His students argue over evidence, not opinions, and every claim they cite has a page number.

Dr Amina, a historian, switches to Research depth, filters the Hijrah scenes by source and status, downloads the claims behind them, and checks whether the route stops match the gazetteer. Her correction goes into the review queue with her name on it.

A design grammar without depiction

Nobody has codified how to present a sacred history immersively without figures. We can, and publish it for others to use:

The lightThe Prophet ﷺ, warm and at the centre, never a node and never a figure (round 3, H4).
LightsPeople, with brightness for generation (T4). Names in Arabic and transliteration.
StonesNamed as present, with no side drawn until claims and scholars decide (X1). They turn to stars only on a recorded conversion.
AbsenceUnlocated places are dashed, and no road is drawn where the text gives only an order. Gaps are shown, not filled.
ChainsNarrators rising from the event toward the book: the isnād made visible.
SoundWind, night, distance; recited source text by a human reader. No instruments by default, following the scholars' ruling.

What not to build

TemptingWhy not
Generated reconstructions of scenes or people (world models such as Genie 3, video models, text-to-3D)They invent architecture, dress and figures and cannot be traced to a source; they break both "no pixel without provenance" and "no depiction". At most, labelled mood backdrops with no people, and only after scholars agree.
AI personas of the Prophet ﷺ or the CompanionsThe clearest red line in the research: removed by Meta, criticised wherever tried, and contrary to the standing rules.
A headset-first productExpeditions shows where that leads. WebXR from the same scenes costs almost nothing; a dedicated app does not.
Leaderboards, streak pressure, points for worshipThey harm motivation and exam scores and raise concerns about sincerity.
Real-time voice chat for everyoneAbout $0.04–0.11 per minute; it would consume a donation budget. Pre-recorded human voice is cheaper and better.
A metaverse twin or NFT "heritage"Dated quickly where tried; adds cost and no learning.

Two rules make this safe. First, nothing in the experience is typed by hand that could be a claim; build scripts refuse a scene whose text does not resolve to claims, as the prototype's build does. Second, the site works fully with the companion switched off; if funding or safety requires it, AI can stop without breaking learning.

Roadmap

Four phases, each ending at a gate decided on evidence. Islamic dates are approximate.

A · Prove it

November 2026 to January 2027 · 12 weeks
  • Hijrah World beta from the prototype (N1), with the designer
  • Moments in the ontology; extraction pass over the Hijrah sections (N2)
  • Place pass for the Hijrah and Makkah (N3)
  • Memory engine and sky (N4); projector and circle mode (N5)
  • Scholars review the Hijrah scenes, about 300 claims (N7)
  • Pilot 1 with the class (E1 in round 3) and a think-aloud study
Gate: delayed recall at 1 week beats a reading-page control by d ≥ 0.3; 30 fps on a $150 Android phone; scholars approve the scene format.

B · Grow the world

February to July 2027 · Ramadan 1448 soft launch
  • Makkah years, Badr and Uḥud as worlds (about 40 scenes)
  • Companion v1 behind published adversarial tests (X1)
  • Voice and sound (X2); Arabic interface
  • Investigations (X3); Hijrah trail companion pilot (X6)
  • Pilot 2: a randomised trial, about 85 per arm
Gate: retention holds at 1 month; zero unsupported statements in the companion audit; teachers return for a second session.

C · The whole Sīra

August 2027 to 2028 · public launch around Rabīʿ al-Awwal 1449
  • Every dated event as a scene, 570–632, with time scrubbing (L1)
  • Multi-source disagreements (X4)
  • Urdu and Malayalam (L4); open benchmark and research API (L5)
  • WebXR tier (L3)
Gate: published learning results and error rate; a versioned DOI release (round 3, W7).

D · Beyond the Sīra

2029 onward
  • The Rāshidūn and the first generations
  • Isnād geography: knowledge travelling from Madinah to Kufa, Nishapur, Cordoba and Malabar (L2)
  • Kerala as the first regional study (round 3, D3)
  • Mastery credentials with teachers (L6)
Gate: the master vision in the README, built one evidenced world at a time.

Experiments before we invest

Small, cheap tests of the riskiest assumptions. Each has a pass mark and a stop rule.

Sample sizes: a two-arm trial needs about 64 per arm for d = 0.5 at 80% power, about 85 with attrition; a within-person design across two units needs about 34 (d = 0.5) to 90 (d = 0.3). Preregister, with recall at one month as the primary outcome.

Decisions for you

  1. Approve the reframe. The graph is the engine; the first product is the Hijrah World, built from the prototype; one build serves every device tier.
